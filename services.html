Services

Helping Your Business Work Better

Every business develops systems for getting work done. As the business grows, those systems don't always grow with it. Processes become more complicated, manual work piles up, information gets scattered, and employees create workarounds just to keep things moving.

I help identify where that friction is coming from and build practical systems around how your business and your people actually work.

You don't have to know which service you need before reaching out. You can start with the problem.

01

Operational Audits

Find out what's actually slowing your business down.

Sometimes you know something isn't working, but you don't know exactly where the problem is.

Work takes longer than it should. Things fall through the cracks. Employees repeat the same tasks, information is difficult to find, or a process that used to work no longer supports the way your business operates today.

An operational audit looks at how the work actually happens, from the people involved and the tools they use to the handoffs, decisions, and processes connecting everything together.

I'll map the current state, identify bottlenecks and unnecessary complexity, and look for the root causes behind the problems you're experiencing.

What I Look At

  • Workflow and process review
  • Bottleneck and friction analysis
  • Roles, responsibilities, and handoffs
  • Tool and system usage
  • Duplicate or unnecessary work
  • Communication and information flow
  • Opportunities for automation or standardization
  • Prioritized recommendations for improvement

You'll walk away with a clearer understanding of what isn't working, why it's happening, and what I recommend changing.

The audit can stand on its own, or we can use the findings to determine which improvements you'd like me to implement.

02

Process Design

Build a better way for work to get done.

A process shouldn't depend on someone remembering what happens next, chasing down missing information, or figuring out the same problem every time the work comes through.

I design and redesign processes so there is a clear path from beginning to end.

That means looking beyond individual tasks and understanding the entire workflow: what starts the process, what information is needed, who is responsible for each step, where decisions and approvals happen, how work moves between people or teams, and what happens when something doesn't go according to plan.

What I Design

  • Current-state and future-state workflow mapping
  • Intake and request processes
  • Approval workflows
  • Roles and ownership
  • Team handoffs
  • Scheduling and planning processes
  • Removing unnecessary steps
  • Identifying missing steps or controls
  • Designing around existing tools and constraints

The goal is not to make a process more complicated in the name of being organized.

It's to create a system that is clear, practical, repeatable, and easier for the people using it.

03

Workflow Automation

Let technology handle the work that doesn't need a person.

Manual work isn't automatically bad. But when someone repeatedly copies information between systems, creates the same thing over and over, tracks down information that already exists somewhere else, or spends hours completing predictable tasks, there's usually an opportunity to build something better.

I look for repetitive work that can be simplified or automated using the tools you already have, integrations between systems, or lightweight custom solutions when needed.

Because of my technical background, I can look at the problem from both sides: how the business needs to operate and what the technology can realistically do.

What I Can Automate

  • Repetitive tasks
  • Connections between tools and systems
  • Information movement between platforms
  • Automated notifications and handoffs
  • Self-service tools
  • Form and intake automation
  • Duplicate data entry reduction
  • Custom lightweight solutions

And sometimes the answer is not automation.

If the underlying process is broken, automating it just creates a faster broken process. I'll recommend fixing the process first when that's the better solution.

The goal is simple: use technology where it saves time, reduces errors, and makes work easier.

04

SOPs & Documentation

Turn what people know into a system everyone can use.

Important business knowledge has a habit of ending up in someone's head.

One employee knows how a process works. Another has their own checklist. Instructions are buried in old emails or messages. New employees learn by asking whoever happens to be available. And when the person who knows how everything works isn't there, the process slows down or stops completely.

I turn that knowledge into documentation people can actually use.

That means understanding how the work is performed, organizing the information clearly, and documenting it in a way that makes the process easier to follow and repeat.

What I Create

  • Standard operating procedures
  • Process documentation
  • Step-by-step instructions
  • Checklists
  • Role and responsibility documentation
  • Intake requirements
  • Handoff procedures
  • Templates
  • Internal reference guides
  • Onboarding and training documentation

I'm not interested in creating a giant manual that gets saved somewhere and never opened again.

Documentation should be easy to find, easy to understand, and useful while someone is actually doing the work.

05

Systems & Team Alignment

When fixing one process isn't enough.

As businesses grow, operational problems rarely stay isolated.

Processes begin overlapping. Teams develop their own ways of working. Tools get added without considering how they fit with everything else. Responsibilities become unclear. Information lives in multiple places. Employees create workarounds to compensate for gaps in the system.

Eventually, fixing one workflow at a time doesn't solve the larger problem.

Systems & Team Alignment is a broader operational engagement focused on how your people, processes, tools, and information work together.

I'll look across the operation to identify disconnects, clarify responsibilities and handoffs, redesign workflows, reduce unnecessary complexity, and create systems that better support the business as a whole.

May Include One or More Of:

Operational Audits
Process Design
Workflow Automation
SOPs & Documentation

The exact work depends on what your business actually needs. We may discover that some systems already work perfectly well and shouldn't be touched, while other areas need to be redesigned entirely.

This isn't about changing everything. It's about making sure everything that needs to work together actually does.
